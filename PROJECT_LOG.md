## Problem

Sometimes it can be tough to track every administrative document, procedure and deadline you need as a university student. The main reason is that so many documents are spread across university webpages, PDFs or emails, making it difficult and time-consuming to find the correct information. This problem often occurs whenever students face administrative situations, such as semester registration, Erasmus mobility or requests for specific documents.

In addition, the administrative offices are overloaded by repetitive questions from different students alongside their day-to-day workload. This can delay their answers and potentially leave students with less time to complete their deadlines, or even make them miss certain deadlines.

## Who are the Users?

- **Users:** University students, particularly those dealing with Erasmus/mobility, semester registration or other administrative procedures.
- **Potential customer and payer:** The university or its international office, if the service reduces repetitive enquiries and improves access to information.
- **Initial target segment:** The university reports over 7200+ students across Bachelor, Master, PhD and Medicine, and 643 administrative and financial staff. This is the potential institutional reach, not the number of students confirmed to need or use the product.

**Sources:**

## Existing Alternatives

As of right now, students must rely completely on themselves: email, the University webpage, and contacting the University administration.

## Value Proposition

For university students who need help navigating administrative procedures, UniGuide AI is an AI-powered administrative guidance assistant that identifies relevant documents, steps and deadlines using official university information.

## Why AI is Needed

AI is needed to automate certain repetitive tasks and respond to the same questions repeatedly. It would also be faster for both student and office, and more flexible.

## Who Pays?

Our initial thought is that the university would offer this service, as it includes official university information. By offering it to their students, it would help the administrative offices deal with fewer repetitive questions, and students would wait less time for documents to reach them.

## Two Interviews

### Interview 1

- **Role:** Business Administration student, 5th semester (returned from Erasmus last semester)
- **Date:** 9.10.2026
- **Length:** 20 min

**Notes:**
1. Deals with mobility (Erasmus last semester) and with semester registration every semester.
2. Last case: registering for the 5th semester, the portal showed the 4th semester. She had to contact the administration to have it changed, and also asked her coordinator, because the information contradicted what she expected.
3. Time: it took 1-2 weeks until she knew what to do, about 2 weeks for her last registration.
4. Cost: nothing money-wise, "only time".
5. Stress 6/10: she did not want to be late for registration since classes were starting and did not want the university to think she had failed her last semester.
6. Workarounds: asks friends, emails to be sure, searches online for documents, and uses AI for the Erasmus part. *"Each one helps in its own way but is often not enough to solve an issue by itself, there is always some work or research to do afterwards."*
7. What she would change: information that is more condensed and specific, with everything in one place.

### Interview 2

- **Role:** Applied Information student, 1st semester
- **Date:** 9.10.2026
- **Length:** 15 min

**Notes:**
- So far only the registration and one deadline: his registration is his only real "search and wait" experience at the university for the moment.
- Time: the whole registration took about 4 weeks to be accepted, and he had to redo his document twice because another document was needed.
- Sources: mostly friends, the rest from the website. The teacher did not explain how to send an assignment so the class group had to ask.
- Moodle information is a bit confusing: notifications mix homework, teacher messages, and events or news.
- Cost: no spending money-wise.
- Stress about 5/10: a teacher emailed asking whether he really wanted to enroll in that course, which stressed him.
- Workarounds: a bit of everything. AI tools for the general part, but for specifics he asks friends more often.
- What he would change: quicker, easier access to the student guichet, which would be "so much better".
